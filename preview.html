Articles

Lesson 4B — 25 questions

Gap Fill

I saw ___ mouse in the kitchen.

Answer: a

New singular noun, first mention → a/an. 'Mouse' starts with a consonant sound → a.

He's ___ artist.

Answer: an

Jobs always take a/an: he's a doctor, she's an artist — never 'he's artist'.

Have you ever seen ___ Sun rise over the ocean?

Answer: the

Only one exists → the. The Sun, the Moon, the internet.

This is ___ most beautiful beach I've ever visited.

Answer: the

Superlative adjective → always the: the best, the tallest, the most beautiful.

We stopped to admire ___ River Danube on our trip.

Answer: the

Water names (rivers, seas, oceans) always take the — but most lakes don't: Lake Como, no article.

He was ___ first person to climb the mountain without oxygen.

Answer: the

The first, the only, the last, the same → always the.

Error Spot

I saw an mouse in the kitchen.

Correction: a

'Mouse' starts with a consonant sound, so use 'a': 'I saw a mouse in the kitchen.'

He works as a engineer in Berlin.

Correction: an

'Engineer' starts with a vowel sound, so use 'an': 'He works as an engineer in Berlin.'

That's a most amazing view I've ever seen.

Correction: the

Superlatives always take 'the': 'That's the most amazing view I've ever seen.'

Scientists are studying a Sun's effect on climate.

Correction: the

Only one Sun exists, so it takes 'the': 'Scientists are studying the Sun's effect on climate.'

I've always wanted to visit a United Kingdom.

Correction: the

Multi-word country names take 'the': 'I've always wanted to visit the United Kingdom.'

We walked along a River Thames yesterday.

Correction: the

Rivers always take 'the': 'We walked along the River Thames yesterday.'

Multiple Choice

Which sentence is correct? (job with a/an)

Answer: He's an artist.

Jobs always need a/an, and the choice between them depends on the sound that follows.

Which sentence is correct? (general statement about a group)

Answer: Elephants are bigger than lions.

General statement about a whole group → plural noun, no article: Elephants are bigger than lions.

Which sentence is correct? (city name)

Answer: I live in Paris.

Most towns, cities and countries → no article: I live in Paris, I visited Japan.

Which sentence is correct? (multi-word country name)

Answer: She moved to the United States last year.

Country name is plural or has extra words (United, Republic, Kingdom) → the. Otherwise → no article.

Which sentence is correct? (sport)

Answer: He plays football every weekend.

Sports and games → no article: I play tennis, he plays football.

Which sentence is correct? (superlative)

Answer: This is the tallest building in the city.

Superlative form → always the: the tallest, the best, the most beautiful.

Which sentence is correct? (defining expression)

Answer: She's the only person who remembered my birthday.

The only, the first, the last, the same → always the, never a/an or no article.

Word Transform

I bought ___ (a/an) umbrella yesterday because it started raining.

Answer: an

It's the sound that follows, not the letter: an umbrella, an hour, but a university.

This dictionary is ___ (a/an) useful tool for learning new words.

Answer: a

Check the sound, not the spelling: a useful tool, a university, a European city — all consonant sounds despite the vowel letter.

Look at ___ (a/the) moon — it's completely full tonight.

Answer: the

Only one exists → the: the moon, the sky, the internet.

That was ___ (a/the) best meal I've had in years.

Answer: the

Superlative form of an adjective → always the.

We're planning a trip to ___ (a/the) Netherlands this summer.

Answer: the

Plural or multi-word country names → the: the Netherlands, the Philippines, the United Kingdom.

She's applying for ___ (a/an) job as a graphic designer.

Answer: a

New singular noun, consonant sound → a. First mention, vowel sound → an.
